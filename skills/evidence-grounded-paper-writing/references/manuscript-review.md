# Manuscript review

## Read for the article's central inference

Identify the article type, target reader, and most consequential claim. Compare what the abstract and conclusions promise with what the methods, results, or synthesis actually establish. Follow that claim through its sources, figures, and tables. Distinguish evidence of a problem from missing reporting. If carrying out a formal external review, follow the applicable confidentiality and AI-use rules before using tools on review material.

## Check the design where it affects the claim

- Find the independent unit and how data were grouped, paired, or repeated. In predictive studies, relate splitting and preprocessing to the use case. Check whether information crosses validation boundaries through repeated units, near-duplicates, feature selection, fitted transformations, or tuning. Distinguish documented overlap from a risk the current reporting leaves open.
- Compare the outcome definition, reference values, baseline, metrics, and uncertainty with the intended application. A ranking metric, threshold-specific performance, and calibration support different claims. A summary test error describes performance under its test design. An interval for an individual case requires additional assumptions or validation.
- Scrutinize mechanism and causality claims. Feature importance, a low-dimensional pattern, or an observational association can suggest hypotheses and identify dependencies. Stronger inference depends on the design or additional observations that distinguish alternatives. Check whether evidence such as timing, covariate adjustment, controls, a direct measurement, or independent replication was actually supplied, as appropriate.
- Check cross-component coherence. Match statements, values, sample counts, units, scope, axis labels, captions, and source credit between prose and exhibits. Test a table title against all rows and a figure caption against each panel. Verify detailed citations using the [literature and citations guide](literature-and-citations.md) when applicable.

## Give a review the author can act on

Prioritize issues by how they affect the central inference, reproducibility, or readability. For substantial points, identify the location, quote the smallest necessary text, explain the consequence, and propose a proportionate remedy. Distinguish a request for clarification, a narrower claim, verification of a record, and work that would require additional analysis. Include section-level reasoning when issues recur across paragraphs. Keep small stylistic changes separate if the requested output is a reviewer report.

When independent review is authorized and valuable, have the reviewer start from the manuscript or requested scope and evidence. Check findings before acting on them. State which sources, figures, or analyses you could verify and which were unavailable.
