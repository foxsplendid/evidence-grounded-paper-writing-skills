# Literature and citations

## Search for studies that can change the argument

Define the question and the kinds of evidence it needs, such as a direct comparator, an independent replication, a contradictory study, or a more representative external test. Translate the concepts into synonyms and database terms. Search resources available in the session and use backward and forward citations from relevant studies where possible. For a currency check, record the date and sources actually searched, including any material access limits. When using a supplied export, distinguish screening that export from running a database query.

Select work by relevance, study design, directness, source quality, and the user's criteria. Check for duplicate versions or reused datasets before treating records as independent evidence. If a useful paper is inaccessible, provide its bibliographic details and verified DOI or source link, along with the specific question the full text would resolve.

## Synthesize around comparisons

Group sources by what they can answer. Record differences in population or material, measurement, reference group, conditions, outcome, and validation. Within comparable evidence, state where studies agree or differ. If results diverge, bring forward the design differences that can be demonstrated and mark proposed explanations as hypotheses. Explain why a study that uses a different comparison remains informative, or leave it out of the comparison.

Reflect the review type. A narrative review can show its scope and selection logic and acknowledge gaps. A systematic review planned or reported as such needs a traceable search, eligibility, screening, extraction, and synthesis process and applicable reporting guidance. Identify records or steps that are missing before writing any claim about procedures performed.

## Verify wording against source material

- Metadata verifies bibliographic identity. Abstracts support only what they explicitly report. A figure panel, table, methods section, or supplement may be needed for exact values, labels, units, data preparation, and comparison groups. Record the precise location when verifying a consequential claim.
- Distinguish directly measured results from model estimates, simulated inputs, indirect proxies, and a primary paper's proposed interpretation. Keep performance values attached to their test design, denominator, threshold, or dataset. Resolve mismatches against the original source and retain uncertainty when the available material cannot.
- Resolve DOI and publication versions where needed. Verify both directions between in-text citations and the bibliography, including figures and tables. Check names, years and suffixes, author order as needed for the intended style, reference type, publication status, and linked identifiers against reliable metadata.
- Apply the requested citation style using the current journal guidance when relevant. Mark unverified details explicitly. Exclude invented references, links, source locations, and findings.

An audit should state which claims were checked, the evidence level reached, any correction needed, and which missing material would settle an open item.
