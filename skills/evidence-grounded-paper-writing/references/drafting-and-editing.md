# Drafting and editing

## Build the paragraph around its job

Choose the unit of organization appropriate to the genre.

- **Original research.** Explain the unresolved comparison or question, give the reader enough design detail to interpret the result, and separate what was observed from the explanation offered. Where the venue separates results and discussion, preserve that distinction.
- **Narrative review.** Organize sections around problems, competing explanations, or conditions across studies. Use case detail to resolve a comparison or change the scope of the synthesis. End a section where the evidence yields a conclusion or identifies the next uncertainty.
- **Systematic review.** Use the actual protocol, eligibility and screening record, appraisal, and synthesis. Consult applicable reporting guidance for the article type. Surface missing documentation and unresolved discrepancies to the author.

Sequence the argument so the next paragraph can use what the reader just learned. Establish comparable terms before comparing results. Follow a paragraph about performance with independent validation when the next claim concerns transfer. Carry over a substantive reference such as a comparator, condition, or unresolved explanation when crossing sections. Use transitions to express that relationship in specific terms.

Keep figures and tables where they make a comparison, support an estimate, or expose a relevant limitation more clearly than the text can. In prose, explain the inference the figure supplies. In the caption, give the information needed to read it, including the actual scope and source where applicable. Check whether the same wording serves different purposes before repeating it.

## Polish within the requested scope

Work from larger logical problems to local sentences when both are requested. For local polishing, keep the design, claims, support, citations, exact quantities, and important qualifiers fixed. Flag a substantive ambiguity separately and explain what evidence or author choice would resolve it.

- Replace filler or vague references with a precise subject, comparison, or measured quantity. If a sentence only tells the reader to consider the broader context, name the particular conditions that affect interpretation, or remove the sentence when it adds nothing.
- Put known material near the start of a sentence when it helps connect the thought. Let sentence length vary with the content. Use direct verbs and adjust heavily nested clauses that conceal the study object or result. Preserve the author's disciplinary terminology and meaningful differences among terms.
- In Chinese, supply the scientific noun when abbreviation or an omitted subject creates ambiguity. Split overlong chains of modifiers and keep reference subjects visible. In English, check information order, attachments, and parallel comparisons. When translating, preserve the original level of certainty and the function of citations.
- Preserve meaningful hedging. A finding described for one test, population, or set of conditions keeps that scope. Distinguish statistical association, predictive performance, and evidence about a process when choosing verbs.

Check the revised text against the input for changes in meaning, numbers, units, citations, and scope. For a representative edit, explain the choice only if requested or if the author needs to resolve an ambiguity.
